How it works

A palette that follows the light, not a setting.

Every color is decided by one question: what does the light look like right now?

It checks the time and your location, finds the sun, and sets three things — how light or dark, what color, how vivid. From those it builds every color at once: backgrounds, text, buttons, borders, all in harmony.

It shifts continuously, too gradually to catch — bright and cool when the sun is high so you can focus; warm and dim after sunset so your eyes and your sleep aren't fighting a glowing screen.

Want your own color in the mix? Slide it in — it stays gentle at night no matter what you pick.

Why it works

Four ideas, one continuous function.

Solar-accurate

Anchors track real sunrise, sunset, golden hour, and solar noon for your location — IST fallback when permission is denied.

Perceptually smooth

All interpolation in OKLCH, never sRGB or HSL. Hues travel the wheel without muddy mid-greys or sudden kinks.

Yours to tint

One brand hue plus a blend slider. Comfort (lightness, chroma) stays locked to the solar clock — only the hue moves.

Readable at every hour

Text colors are solved against the live background to clear WCAG AA at every minute of the day, every day of the year.

Footnote

The crossover band, made tolerable.

Around dawn and sunset the background passes through mid-grey, where no text color can clear AA. The engine emits a meta.crossoverProximity signal (0–1, peaks at the flip minute); a translucent scrim fades in for the ~17–24 minutes when color alone can't carry legibility, then fades back out.

The text polarity flip itself is irreducible — any continuous lightness path through the crossover must cross zero-contrast grey. The scrim is the engineered fallback.
